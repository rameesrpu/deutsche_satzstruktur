# Medical content — not yet checked by a doctor

**Fall „Bauchschmerzen“ (case "abdominal pain"): review sheet (plan 5b, Task 3)**

Klinik → Anamnese → *Gespräch* → Bauchschmerzen. Level B1. Data: `data/medical/faelle/bauchschmerzen.json` (version 4). Invented patient, invented teaching content — not medical advice. The suspected diagnosis is acute appendicitis.

How the interview works: for each step the learner sees the section and three questions (one right, two wrong). After the right question the patient answers (shown and spoken). Then three notes: she taps the one that follows from the answer, and it goes into the Anamnesebogen. The letter (*Arztbrief*), the documentation and the presentation are used by later exercises (Arztbrief sorting, FSP practice run).

The interview has 13 steps (the plan said 10–12; one more so that the last period, bowel movements and urination are asked).

## Arztbrief (letter), by section

### Diagnosen (Diagnoses)

- Akute Appendizitis — Acute appendicitis

### Anamnese (History)

- Die Patientin stellte sich mit seit dem Vorabend bestehenden Bauchschmerzen vor. — The patient presented with abdominal pain that had started the evening before.
- Die Schmerzen begannen periumbilikal und wanderten im Verlauf in den rechten Unterbauch. — The pain started around the navel and later moved to the right lower abdomen.
- Begleitend bestanden Übelkeit, Appetitlosigkeit, einmaliges Erbrechen sowie Fieber bis 38,2 °C; Stuhlgang und Miktion waren unauffällig. — There was also nausea, loss of appetite, one episode of vomiting and fever up to 38.2 °C; bowel movements and urination were normal.
- Die letzte Menstruation lag zwei Wochen zurück. — The last menstrual period was two weeks ago.

### Befund (Findings)

- Klinisch zeigte sich ein Druckschmerz über dem McBurney-Punkt mit positivem Loslassschmerz. — On examination there was tenderness over McBurney's point with positive rebound tenderness.
- Laborchemisch fanden sich eine Leukozytose von 14,2/nl und ein CRP von 45 mg/l. — Blood tests showed a raised white cell count of 14.2/nl and a CRP of 45 mg/l.
- Das β-HCG war negativ. — The β-HCG was negative.
- Sonografisch stellte sich eine verdickte, nicht komprimierbare Appendix dar. — Ultrasound showed a thickened, non-compressible appendix.

### Therapie und Verlauf (Treatment and course)

- Am Aufnahmetag führten wir eine laparoskopische Appendektomie durch. — On the day of admission we performed a laparoscopic appendectomy.
- Der postoperative Verlauf war komplikationslos. — The course after the operation was without complications.

### Empfehlung (Recommendation)

- Wir empfehlen eine Wundkontrolle und den Fadenzug am 10. postoperativen Tag beim Hausarzt. — We recommend a wound check and removal of the stitches by the GP on the 10th day after the operation.
- Für zwei Wochen sollte die Patientin auf schweres Heben und Sport verzichten. — For two weeks the patient should avoid heavy lifting and sport.

**Model:**

> Ich möchte Ihnen Frau Sophie Brandt vorstellen, eine 24-jährige Studentin. Sie kam heute wegen starker Bauchschmerzen zu uns. Die Schmerzen bestehen seit gestern Abend, nehmen zu und haben sich vom Nabel in den rechten Unterbauch verlagert. Begleitend bestehen Übelkeit, Appetitlosigkeit, einmaliges Erbrechen und Fieber bis 38,2 °C. Stuhlgang und Wasserlassen sind unauffällig, die letzte Regelblutung war vor zwei Wochen. Vorerkrankungen und Voroperationen hat sie keine. An Medikamenten nimmt sie nur die Pille. Allergien sind nicht bekannt. Mein Verdacht ist eine akute Appendizitis. Differenzialdiagnostisch kommen eine Eileiterschwangerschaft, eine Ovarialzyste oder ein Harnwegsinfekt in Frage. *I would like to present Ms Sophie Brandt, a 24-year-old student. She came to us today because of severe abdominal pain. The pain has been there since yesterday evening, is getting worse and has moved from the navel to the right lower abdomen. She also has nausea, loss of appetite, one episode of vomiting and fever up to 38.2 °C. Bowel movements and urination are normal, her last period was two weeks ago. She has no previous illnesses or operations. As medication she only takes the pill. No known allergies. My suspected diagnosis is acute appendicitis. Differential diagnoses are an ectopic pregnancy, an ovarian cyst or a urinary tract infection. I would arrange a physical examination, blood tests with a full blood count and CRP, a urine test with a pregnancy test and an abdominal ultrasound, and I would call in the surgical colleagues.*

## What to check

1. **Medical plausibility:** Is this a typical appendicitis presentation for a 24-year-old woman (pain moving from the navel to the right lower abdomen, nausea, anorexia, one vomit, 38.2 °C, normal stool and urine, last period two weeks ago, Leukozyten 14.2/nl, CRP 45 mg/l, β-HCG negative, sonography)? Are the differential diagnoses (ectopic pregnancy, ovarian cyst, urinary tract infection) and the planned tests the ones you would name? Is the treatment and recommendation (laparoscopic appendectomy; wound check and stitches out on day 10 at the GP; two weeks without heavy lifting and sport) what a German clinic would write?
2. **Interview:** Are these the questions you would ask, in this order? Is anything important missing? Is the last-period question in the right section (here: Vegetative Anamnese; some forms have a separate gynaecological history)? Is each "wrong" question really wrong at that point, and each wrong note clearly not what the patient said?
3. **German a doctor would use:** Do the notes, the letter sentences, the documentation and the presentation sound like a German doctor (Arztbrief style, "Z. n.", "laborchemisch", "sonografisch", "Miktion", "familienanamnestisch")?
